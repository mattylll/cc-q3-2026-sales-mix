THE ADDITIONAL ANALYSIS

One distance, four visible contributions

Calculate each property type's share from its count and the total recorded count. Take the difference between B and A, add the absolute differences, then halve the result. This is total variation expressed on a 0-to-100 percentage-point scale.

Mix distance = ½ × Σ |share B − share A|

The division by two avoids counting the same redistribution twice. If ten percentage points move from detached homes to flats, one share falls by ten and the other rises by ten. Their absolute differences total twenty; the mix distance is ten. Zero means identical four-type shares. One hundred would mean no shared category mass.

In the default example, the London and South East flat share is 13.90 percentage points higher than the national share. Its other three shares are lower, so that positive difference equals the whole mix distance. This is an allocation comparison between two proportions. It does not mean that 13.90% of transactions were replaced, that prices are 13.90% higher, or that one geography is a poor investment.

A small distance does not certify a complete quarter

The national four-type mix changes by only 1.94 percentage points between recorded Q3 2025 and Q3 2026. Yet the latter extract has 61,717 records compared with 210,888 a year earlier. Its 29.27% count ratio cannot measure how complete Q3 will eventually be. Similar broad shares can coexist with registration lag, different locations within a region, different floor areas and different new-build coverage.

HM Land Registry's Price Paid Data guidance explains that transaction and registration dates differ and recent counts are incomplete. Our tool uses the fixed aggregates in the Q3 sales-registration and property-mix dataset on Kaggle. It does not refresh those records or estimate unregistered sales.

No price adjustment is being calculated

Four categories cannot control for size, condition, tenure, location or sale timing. A small score establishes only that these four recorded shares are similar. There is no threshold for a “safe” comparator. Medians cannot be made constant-quality by averaging category shares, and the aggregates here cannot reconstruct a matched-property valuation.

USE IN A DEVELOPMENT APPRAISAL

Separate a descriptive comparator from an exit valuation

Construction Capital's London and South East Q3 property report places regional sales evidence in its broader market context. This tool adds a composition check: record the geography, quarter, source edition, category counts and gaps beside any comparison you cite.

For a development exit, the next step still requires scheme-specific comparable evidence and an appropriate valuation. The Development Finance guide and service overview explains where valuation, project costs, consent and funding structure sit in that process. A mix-distance score cannot determine the value a lender will accept or the terms it will offer.

Keep the other Q3 evidence in its own lane. The Hugging Face planning dataset tests how stage and proposal definitions alter a pipeline claim. The Harvard charge-activity dataset describes recorded security registrations. The companion lender concentration explorer makes the difference between lender headcounts and activity distribution visible. Those series cannot be joined to these aggregates to trace an individual scheme from consent through funding to sale.

When a median is the wrong summary

For a complementary check on price comparisons, our regional-median methods note shows why regional counts and medians cannot reconstruct a national median. It includes 15 quarterly comparisons and an explicit synthetic example.
